# Shorts scripts v2: playbook and next three scripts (2 Nov 2026)

Why: ep25-ep28 were "not even average" (owner). The visuals are now fixed with real NASA video
(`short_sun_v2.py`). This fixes the other half: scripts were one common fact restated, with a summary ending.

## Playbook (every script must pass all 7 before it's built)

1. **Frame-1 claim.** The first line is the most surprising sentence in the video, and the first frame
   shows it (picture + 2-line label). No setup, no "Did you know".
2. **A fact people half-know, then a turn.** Open with something familiar-sounding, then at ~60% flip it
   ("But...") to something most viewers have never heard. The turn is the reason to finish.
3. **Every line opens a question the next line answers.** If a line could be cut without losing
   anything, cut it.
4. **Concrete, picturable numbers.** "An Olympic pool every half hour", not "kilograms per second".
   One number per line, max.
5. **Payoff is the last word.** No summary line, no "so next time you...". The last frame dissolves to the
   first (loop).
6. **45-60 spoken words, 16-21 s.** Short sentences; Andrew voice at +2 to +8%.
7. **Every claim sourced** (below each script), with numbers rounded to what the sources agree on.
   If sources disagree, say the safe version ("about", "maybe", "thousands of").

Rubric used below (1-5): Hook, Turn, Payoff, Facts, Footage. Build only if all are 4+.

## A. "Jupiter's Great Red Spot Is an Impostor" (replaces ep27 script)

| # | Voice | Picture (real footage / PD) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | This storm on Jupiter is an impostor. | Juno/Hubble Jupiter, spot circled; label "THIS STORM IS / AN IMPOSTOR" |
| 2 | In 1665, astronomers spotted a giant spot right here. | Old Jupiter drawing, year "1665" |
| 3 | For centuries, we thought it was the same storm. | Push into the spot (JunoCam rotation animation PIA22178, if licence OK) |
| 4 | But that spot vanished in 1713, and for over a hundred years, nothing. | Spot fades out; counter 1713 → 1831 |
| 5 | Today's storm only appeared around 1831. A completely different one. | Juno close-up, label "NEW STORM: ~190 YEARS OLD" |
| 6 | And it's shrinking, so we might watch this one die too. | Pull back to whole Jupiter, dissolve to frame 1 |

~62 words. Turn: line 4. Payoff: "die too."
Rubric: Hook 5 (impostor), Turn 5, Payoff 4, Facts 4 (one 2024 study; worded as "around", "might"), Footage 4.

Sources:
- Sánchez-Lavega et al., Geophysical Research Letters, 16 Jun 2024: Cassini's "Permanent Spot" seen
  1665-1713, then lost; today's spot first seen 1831, likely a different storm, ~190 years old.
  [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/jupiter/is-jupiters-great-red-spot-an-impostor-giant-storm-may-not-be-the-original-one-discovered-350-years-ago),
  [Sky at Night](https://skyatnightmagazine.com/news/jupiter-great-red-spot-not-same-storm-observed-by-cassini),
  [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/space/jupiters-great-red-spot-is-190-years-old/)
- Shrinking (and growing taller): [Planetary Society 2021](https://www.planetary.org/articles/why-jupiter-great-red-spot-changing-shape)
- Footage to check licence: [PIA22178 Great Red Spot Rotation](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/great-red-spot-rotation-animation)

## B. "The Moon Is Stealing Our Eclipses" (new; replaces "The Moon Is Leaving Us")

| # | Voice | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Total solar eclipses are going to end forever. | Real 2017/2024 totality footage (NASA); label "ECLIPSES WILL / END FOREVER" |
| 2 | Right now, the Moon is just big enough to cover the Sun. | Moon sliding over the Sun, diamond ring |
| 3 | But it's drifting away, about four centimetres every year. | LRO / Apollo Moon footage, "+3.8 cm / YEAR" |
| 4 | We measure it with mirrors the Apollo astronauts left behind. | Apollo 11 retroreflector footage, laser beam |
| 5 | It's already slowed our days. Dinosaurs had 23 and a half hour days. | Earth rotating (ISS / DSCOVR), "23.5 HOUR DAY" |
| 6 | In about 600 million years, the Moon won't fit, and Earth sees its last total eclipse. | Eclipse becomes a ring of fire, dissolve to frame 1 |

~70 words: trim line 5 if over 21 s. Turn: lines 3-4. Payoff: "last total eclipse."
Rubric: Hook 5, Turn 4, Payoff 5, Facts 4 ("about 600 million"; estimates 500-650 My), Footage 5.

Sources:
- 3.8 cm/yr from Apollo laser ranging: [NASA GSFC eclipse site](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/ApolloLaser.html),
  [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/longest-laser-ranging-experiment)
- Last total eclipse ~600 My (range 500-650): [Space.com](https://www.space.com/37627-total-solar-eclipse-earth-moon-alignment-future.html),
  [NASA eclipse2017](https://science.gsfc.nasa.gov/attic/eclipse2017.gsfc.nasa.gov/what's-very-last-solar-eclipse.html),
  [Sky & Telescope](https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/astronomy-questions-answers/when-will-the-last-total-solar-eclipse-occur/)
- 23.5 h day 70 Mya (de Winter et al. 2020, rudist clam): [Space.com](https://www.space.com/ancient-clam-fossil-reveals-earth-shorter-day.html),
  [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/paleontology/cretaceous-earth-day-08211.html)

## C. "Saturn's Rings Are Younger Than the Dinosaurs" (a new Short, not an edit of ep26)

| # | Voice | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Saturn's rings are younger than the dinosaurs. | Cassini Saturn footage, label "YOUNGER THAN / THE DINOSAURS" |
| 2 | Saturn is four and a half billion years old. | Whole Saturn, "4,500,000,000 YEARS" |
| 3 | Its rings? Maybe less than a hundred million. | Push into the rings, "< 100,000,000" |
| 4 | Cassini weighed them by diving between the rings and the planet. | Cassini Grand Finale footage/animation |
| 5 | And they're not staying. Ice is raining onto Saturn, an Olympic pool every half hour. | Rings close-up, "1 OLYMPIC POOL / 30 MIN" |
| 6 | So we're lucky. We showed up in the short time Saturn has rings at all. | Pull back to Saturn, dissolve to frame 1 |

~66 words. Turn: line 5. Payoff: "rings at all."
Rubric: Hook 5, Turn 4, Payoff 4, Facts 4 (10-100 My contested; "maybe"), Footage 5 (Cassini).

Sources:
- Rings 10-100 My (Iess et al., Science, Jan 2019): [Space.com](https://www.space.com/43042-saturn-rings-younger-than-dinosaurs-nasa-cassini.html),
  [Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/saturns-rings-are-surprisingly-young/); dissent:
  [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/are-saturns-rings-really-as-young-as-the-dinosaurs-20191121/)
- Ring rain "could fill an Olympic-sized swimming pool ... in half an hour" (O'Donoghue, NASA Goddard,
  Icarus, 17 Dec 2018); rings gone in ~300 My from rain alone, under 100 My counting equatorial infall:
  [NASA release](https://www.nasa.gov/press-release/goddard/2018/ring-rain)
